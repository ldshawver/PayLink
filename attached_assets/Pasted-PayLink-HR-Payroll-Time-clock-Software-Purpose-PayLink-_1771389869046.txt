PayLink HR, Payroll & Time‑clock Software

Purpose.  PayLink is intended to manage employee and independent‑contractor time‑tracking, scheduling, payroll and HR for multiple businesses, including S‑corporations and 501(c)(3) non‑profits.  Below is research on legal requirements and a design blueprint for building the software on Replit.  It covers compliance obligations and outlines modular components and technologies.

1 Legal compliance considerations

1.1 Worker classification and recordkeeping
	•	Employee vs. independent contractor – The Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) does not define “independent contractor”; courts use an economic‑reality test.  Relevant factors include opportunity for profit or loss, investments by the worker and employer, permanence of the relationship, degree of control, whether the work is integral to the business and worker skill/initiative ￼.  Misclassification can lead to liability for minimum wage/overtime, taxes and penalties ￼.
	•	Required records – Employers must keep, for each non‑exempt employee, the worker’s full name, address, Social Security number, sex and occupation, time and day the workweek begins, hours worked each day and week, pay rate, total and overtime earnings, additions/deductions, total wages per pay period, and date of payment ￼.  Payroll records must be retained for 3 years; time cards and wage computations for 2 years ￼.  Employers may use any method of timekeeping as long as it is complete and accurate ￼.
	•	Time rounding rules – The Department of Labor allows employers to record time to the nearest 5 minutes, tenth of an hour or quarter hour, provided that over time the rounding practice does not systematically undercount hours ￼.  Many systems adopt a 7‑minute rule (round to the nearest quarter hour) but must ensure employees receive full compensation ￼.
	•	Meal and rest breaks – Employers must also track breaks.  For example, California requires an unpaid 30‑minute meal break for any work period over five hours and a second break when working more than 10 hours ￼ ￼.  Paid 10‑minute rest breaks must be provided for every four hours worked ￼ and an extra hour of pay is owed when a break is not provided ￼.  State rules vary.

1.2 Payroll taxes and forms
	•	Employee payroll (FICA, FUTA, FITW)
– Employers must withhold federal income tax (FITW) and both the employee and employer shares of FICA (Social Security and Medicare).  The combined FICA rate is 15.3 % (6.2 % × 2 for Social Security up to a wage‑base and 1.45 % × 2 for Medicare); self‑employed individuals pay the combined rate and may deduct the employer portion ￼.
– Employers file Form 941 each quarter to report federal income tax withheld and the employer’s share of Social Security and Medicare taxes ￼.
– Federal unemployment tax (FUTA) applies to employers at 6 % on the first $7,000 of wages; employers that pay state unemployment taxes on time may receive a credit of up to 5.4 %, reducing the net federal rate to 0.6 % ￼.  Employers must deposit FUTA taxes quarterly and file Form 940 ￼.
– Certain employers are exempt.  The IRS states that an organization exempt from income tax under § 501(c)(3) is also exempt from FUTA and cannot waive this exemption ￼, but it must still withhold and deposit FITW and FICA ￼.
	•	Independent contractor payments
– Businesses must obtain a completed Form W‑9 from each contractor to capture the correct name and taxpayer identification number (TIN); the form should be kept on file for four years ￼.
– Payments totaling ≥ $600 in a year ($2,000 for payments after December 31 2025) to a non‑employee for services must be reported on Form 1099‑NEC ￼.  The IRS instructs payers to use Form 1099‑NEC rather than Form 1099‑MISC for non‑employee compensation, and to e‑file if they issue 10 or more information returns ￼.
– If the contractor fails to furnish a valid TIN, backup withholding at 24 % is required, reported on Form 945 ￼.
– Contractors are responsible for their own federal payroll taxes (self‑employment tax).  The self‑employment tax rate is 15.3 % of net earnings; contractors normally pay these quarterly with Form 1040‑ES ￼.
	•	S‑corporation shareholder‑employees
– The IRS requires S‑corps to pay shareholder‑employees reasonable compensation for services before making distributions.  Reasonable compensation cannot exceed the amount received by the shareholder and is based on factors like training, duties, time spent, payments to non‑shareholder employees and what similar businesses pay ￼.
– Distributions in excess of reasonable compensation may be reclassified as wages subject to employment taxes ￼, and S‑corps must report wages on Form W‑2 and deposit the employer share of payroll taxes.  Health‑insurance premiums for >2 % shareholder‑employees must be treated as wages and reported on the W‑2 ￼.
	•	Non‑profit organizations
– Although 501(c)(3) organizations are exempt from FUTA, they must still withhold federal income tax and FICA ￼ ￼.  State unemployment taxes (SUTA) may also apply ￼.
– Non‑profits must file and deposit payroll taxes on the same schedule as for‑profit employers and maintain employee records.

1.3 Other compliance considerations
	•	Overtime rules – Non‑exempt employees must be paid at 1.5× their regular rate for hours worked over 40 in a workweek ￼.  Independent contractors are not entitled to overtime ￼.
	•	Minimum wage – Employers must pay at least the federal minimum wage ($7.25 per hour as of 2026) and comply with state and local minimum wage laws ￼.
	•	Break policies – Employers must comply with state‑specific meal and rest break laws (e.g., California’s 30‑minute meal break after 5 hours and a second break after 10 hours ￼ ￼; 10‑minute paid rest breaks per four hours ￼).  The software should allow creation of state‑specific break policies.

2 Core functional components for PayLink

Based on TimeTrex and ADP feature sets and the legal requirements above, the following modules should be incorporated into PayLink.  Each section lists key functions and compliance tasks.

2.1 Dashboard and navigation
	•	Interchangeable dashlets – The home page should allow customizable widgets (dashlets) such as news, messages, pending requests, exception summaries (missing punches, overtime alerts), schedule summary, “Who’s in/out” and timesheet summary.  Users should be able to drag‑and‑drop or add/remove dashlets.
	•	Multi‑company selector – Since PayLink supports multiple companies, include a selector to switch context.  Ensure each company’s legal entity, tax IDs and payroll settings are isolated.
	•	Role‑based access control – Implement permission groups (admin, manager, employee, contractor).  For each role define allowed menus (HR, payroll, reports) and data they can view or edit.  Use a hierarchical structure for managers to view subordinates’ data.

2.2 Attendance and time‑tracking
	•	Clock‑in/out and timesheets – Provide multiple ways to record hours:
	•	Real‑time punch via web or mobile.  Capture timestamp, location (GPS/IP) and optionally photo for geofencing/biometric verification.
	•	Manual timesheets for contractors who bill by project or who submit weekly hours rather than punching.
	•	Rounding settings – Administrator can choose rounding increments (5, 6, 15 minutes) with a default 7‑minute/quarter‑hour rule.  Ensure rounding is neutral and does not systematically undercount hours ￼.
	•	Exception handling – Flag missed punches, late arrivals or overtime; allow supervisors to add notes or correct entries while maintaining an audit trail.
	•	Accrual balances – Track paid time off (vacation, sick leave) with accrual formulas (e.g., hours per pay period).  Provide dashboards for employees to view balances and request leave.
	•	Break & meal compliance – Provide configurable meal and rest break policies per jurisdiction.  For example, configure a mandatory 30‑minute unpaid meal break after five hours and track whether breaks were taken; generate premium pay (one hour of pay) if not provided ￼.

2.3 Scheduling module
	•	Shift scheduling – Create daily/weekly schedules.  Include recurring schedules and templates.
	•	Availability & time‑off integration – Allow employees and contractors to submit availability and PTO requests.  The scheduler should prevent scheduling when employees are unavailable or when overtime/meal break rules would be violated.
	•	Station/Department assignment – Assign shifts to locations, departments or work stations.  For multi‑company setups, track cross‑company assignments and ensure wages are allocated to the correct entity.

2.4 Employee and contractor management
	•	Profile management – For employees, store personal details, contacts, job titles, wages, pay methods (direct deposit, check), tax information (W‑4 details), employment start/end dates, and group memberships.  For contractors, store W‑9 details (TIN, classification) and payment terms.
	•	Classification & onboarding – Provide a wizard to capture forms (W‑4 for employees; W‑9 for contractors) and classify the worker using the economic‑reality factors (opportunity for profit/loss, investment, control, etc.) ￼.  The classification should be stored and used for compliance.
	•	Employment eligibility & I‑9 – Integrate I‑9 document storage and E‑Verify where needed.
	•	Shareholder employees – In S‑corp mode, flag employees who are >2 % shareholders.  Require entry of reasonable salary amount and track distributions to ensure wages meet IRS reasonable compensation guidelines ￼.

2.5 Company & entity configuration
	•	Company information – Store legal name, EIN, tax classification (C‑corp, S‑corp, LLC, 501(c)(3)), addresses, branches, departments and hierarchy.  Include secondary wage groups and currencies for multi‑state or international operations.
	•	Payroll policies – Define pay periods (weekly, biweekly, semimonthly), overtime policies, meal/break rules, rounding rules, holiday calendars and accrual policies.  Policies should be assignable to groups of employees/contractors.
	•	Tax setup – For each company, enter tax account numbers (federal EIN, state unemployment ID) and specify whether the entity is exempt from FUTA (e.g., 501(c)(3)) ￼.  Define remittance agencies and deposit schedules.

2.6 Payroll processing
	•	Gross‑to‑net calculation – For employees, compute regular, overtime and premium wages; calculate employer and employee portions of FICA, FUTA/SUTA, state/local taxes, garnishments, and voluntary deductions (health insurance, retirement).  For contractors, calculate net pay minus any backup withholding or other contractual deductions; compute self‑employment tax and display on paystub for information (contractors are responsible for paying it themselves ￼).  Provide year‑to‑date totals.
	•	Payroll approval workflow – Allow managers to review and approve pay runs.  Generate payroll register reports and preview paystubs.
	•	Tax filings – Generate and e‑file:
	•	Quarterly Forms 941 for employee payroll taxes ￼.
	•	Form 940 for FUTA when applicable ￼; skip FUTA for 501(c)(3) entities ￼.
	•	State unemployment and income tax filings.
	•	Forms W‑2/W‑3 for employees, including shareholder‑employee health‑insurance premiums treated as wages ￼.
	•	Forms 1099‑NEC for contractors whose payments exceed the threshold ￼, and associated Form 1096 for paper filings.  Include e‑file support (IRIS/FIRE) ￼.
	•	Form 945 for backup withholding when a contractor fails to provide a TIN ￼.
	•	Schedule SE details on paystubs to aid contractors with self‑employment tax.
	•	Check printing and direct deposit – Integrate with check printing to produce branded paystubs, or allow ACH via direct deposit.  Include encryption and tokenization for bank account data.
	•	General ledger integration – Export payroll journal entries to accounting systems (e.g., QuickBooks, Xero).  Provide mapping of wage and tax accounts per company.

2.7 Policies and compliance engine
	•	Policy groups – Implement a flexible rules engine to configure pay codes (regular, overtime, double time), pay formulas, contributing pay codes, shifts, accrual accounts, recurring holidays, schedule policies, rounding policies, meal and break rules, overtime rules, premium pay, exception policies, absence policies and holiday policies.  Each policy should be version‑controlled and effective‑dated.
	•	Audit trail – Maintain an audit log of all changes to employee data, timesheets, schedules, policies and payroll runs.  Provide a report showing who made a change, what fields changed and timestamps.  This is essential for compliance with recordkeeping rules ￼.

2.8 Human‑resources module
	•	Reviews and KPI tracking – Allow managers to perform performance reviews, track key performance indicators (KPIs), qualifications, skills, education, memberships, licenses and languages.  Provide document storage for certifications and expiration reminders.
	•	EEO and diversity – Collect optional ethnic group and demographic data for compliance reports; ensure sensitive personal data is protected and access is limited to authorized HR personnel.  Data should not influence decisions automatically; human review is required.
	•	New‑hire and termination workflows – Automate new‑hire onboarding tasks (offer letters, welcome packets, benefit enrollment) and termination tasks (final paycheck, COBRA notices).  Generate state‑specific new‑hire reporting forms.

2.9 Reporting and analytics
	•	Saved reports – Provide report templates and the ability to save custom filters.
	•	Employee reports – Who’s in/out summary, employee information, audit trail.
	•	Timesheet reports – Schedule summary, timesheet summary, detail, punch summary, accrual balance summary, exception summary.
	•	Payroll reports – Paystub summary, payroll export, general ledger summary, payroll liability.
	•	Tax reports – Tax summary, state unemployment, quarterly Form 941, annual Form 940, Form 1099‑NEC summary and Form W‑2/W‑3 filings.  Provide calculators for self‑employment tax and display the 15.3 % rate with Social Security wage base information ￼.
	•	HR reports – Qualification summary, review summary, EEO/affirmative‑action metrics.  Provide anonymized and aggregated data to reduce the risk of discrimination.

3 Technical architecture and development plan (Replit)

3.1 Technology stack

Replit supports multiple languages and deploys web applications via hosting and repls.  A modern stack that balances productivity and scalability would include:
	1.	Frontend – Use React (JavaScript/TypeScript) with a component library like Material UI or Ant Design for responsive dashboards and forms.  Implement role‑based routing using React Router.  Use context/state management (Redux or Zustand) to manage global states (current company, user session, flash messages).
	2.	Backend – Use Node.js with Express or FastAPI (Python).  Node integrates well with JavaScript front‑end, while Python offers strong libraries (e.g., SQLAlchemy).  Both run smoothly on Replit.  Include RESTful endpoints for each module (employees, contractors, timesheets, payroll, reports).  Use JSON Web Tokens (JWT) for authentication and session management.
	3.	Database – Choose PostgreSQL (via Replit’s built‑in database or external cluster) for relational data (employees, timesheets, payroll records).  Use Sequelize (Node) or SQLAlchemy (Python) for ORM mapping.  Ensure proper indexing for time‑series queries.
	4.	Task queue – Use Redis with BullMQ (Node) or Celery (Python) for background tasks: payroll calculations, report generation and tax form creation.  This prevents blocking the request‑response cycle.
	5.	PDF/Print generation – Use libraries like pdfkit or ReportLab (Python) or Puppeteer (Node) to generate paystubs, checks and tax forms from HTML templates.
	6.	Authentication & security – Implement multi‑factor authentication for administrators.  Store passwords hashed with bcrypt.  Use HTTPS for all communication.  Encrypt sensitive data (SSNs, bank accounts) at rest and in transit.  Comply with privacy regulations (GDPR/CCPA) by providing data‑subject access and deletion tools.
	7.	API integration – For e‑filing forms, integrate with the IRS IRIS portal for 1099 and W‑2 submissions; integrate ACH via a payments API (e.g., Stripe or Plaid) for direct deposit.  For tax rate updates, integrate with a service like Symmetry or Avalara or maintain a rate table updated manually.
	8.	Infrastructure & deployment – Host the application on a Replit private repl or a Virtual Private Server (VPS).  For high availability, containerize the app with Docker and deploy on a VPS provider (e.g., DigitalOcean).  Use NGINX as a reverse proxy and load balancer.
	9.	Version control & CI/CD – Use GitHub integrated with Replit.  Configure GitHub Actions to run unit tests (Jest/PyTest), linting and static analysis.  Deploy to the Replit environment or VPS on merge to main.
	10.	Documentation – Provide API documentation via OpenAPI/Swagger.  Include user manuals and video tutorials within the dashboard.

3.2 Development roadmap
	1.	Planning & requirements – Gather detailed requirements per module.  Create user personas (HR admin, manager, employee, contractor, accountant).  Draft data models and workflows.
	2.	Project setup on Replit – Create a new Replit project.  Set up a monorepo with separate /frontend and /backend directories.  Initialize Git and link to GitHub.  Define environment variables for database credentials, JWT secrets and API keys (do not commit secrets).
	3.	Database schema design – Design tables for: companies, legal entities, branches, departments, permission groups, employees, contractors, timesheets, punches, schedules, policies, pay runs, tax forms, accrual accounts, and audit logs.  Create migration scripts using an ORM.  Include foreign keys and constraints for data integrity.
	4.	User authentication & company selection – Implement login/registration, password reset, two‑factor authentication.  After login, display a company selector if the user belongs to multiple companies.  Use RBAC to restrict access to modules.
	5.	Attendance module – Build endpoints to create/read/update/delete (CRUD) punches and timesheets.  On the front‑end, create clock‑in/out buttons with geolocation and offline capability.  Implement timesheet review screens for supervisors and employees.
	6.	Scheduling module – Build UI for calendar‑based scheduling (use libraries like FullCalendar).  Provide templates for recurring shifts.  Implement constraints (e.g., avoid overlapping shifts and exceeding maximum hours).
	7.	HR module – Develop employee/contractor onboarding forms capturing W‑4/W‑9 details, I‑9 verification and attachments.  Include fields for shareholder status, qualifications and performance metrics.
	8.	Company & policy setup – Build UI to create new companies, assign legal entities, departments and configure payroll policies (rounding, overtime, meal/break).  Use wizard‑style forms to simplify setup.
	9.	Payroll engine – Implement core payroll calculation logic as a service.  Accept pay period start/end dates and compute gross pay, taxes and net pay based on payroll policies.  Include unit tests covering edge cases (overtime, holiday pay, multiple pay rates, contractor calculations with backup withholding).  Provide manual adjustments and amendment capability.
	10.	Reporting & tax forms – Implement PDF templates for paystubs, checks and tax forms (W‑2, W‑3, 940, 941, 1099‑NEC).  Use scheduled tasks to generate quarterly and annual filings.  Provide interactive dashboards and exports (CSV/Excel).  Ensure forms include required fields (employer EIN, employee SSN, wages, withheld taxes) and meet IRS formatting requirements.
	11.	Notifications & workflows – Add email notifications or in‑app messages for approvals (time‑off, timesheets, payroll runs), reminders for filing deadlines (e.g., Form 941 due dates), and alerts for missing punches or policy violations.
	12.	Testing & quality assurance – Conduct functional testing, integration testing and user acceptance testing.  Use dummy data to simulate multi‑company scenarios and ensure separation of data.
	13.	Security & compliance audits – Perform security audits and vulnerability scanning.  Ensure encryption, least‑privilege access, and audit logs.  Validate compliance with FLSA, IRS rules and state regulations.
	14.	Deployment & maintenance – After acceptance, deploy to the VPS.  Set up backups, monitoring (Prometheus/Grafana), and error logging (Sentry).  Schedule regular updates for tax tables and legislation changes.

4 Key compliance features to include
	•	Worker‑classification wizard that guides admins through the FLSA economic‑reality factors to determine employee vs. contractor ￼.  Include checklists and recommended actions (e.g., collect W‑9, W‑4, sign contract).
	•	Record retention and audit trail that automatically retains payroll records for at least 3 years and time records for 2 years ￼.  Provide export options and logs to satisfy DOL audits.
	•	Overtime and minimum wage calculators that enforce FLSA and state rules; highlight when an employee’s rate falls below the highest applicable minimum wage and when overtime thresholds are crossed ￼.
	•	Meal/rest break monitor that tracks compliance with state laws (e.g., California’s meal and rest break requirements) and automatically adds premium pay when breaks are missed ￼.
	•	Tax exemption flags for 501(c)(3) organizations (no FUTA) ￼ and for S‑corp shareholder‑employee wages (health‑insurance premiums included in wages but exempt from FICA/FUTA if part of a qualifying plan ￼).
	•	Auto‑generation of W‑9 and W‑4 forms for contractors and employees.  Provide digital signature and storage of these forms.  Remind contractors to update information each year.
	•	Self‑employment tax estimation on contractor paystubs.  Display the 15.3 % rate and indicate the Social Security wage base ￼.
	•	Backup withholding management – Identify contractors without valid TINs, apply 24 % withholding and generate Form 945 ￼.
	•	Reasonable compensation check – For S‑corp shareholder‑employees, prompt administrators to enter reasonable salary and compare to industry benchmarks.  Warn if distributions exceed wages, referencing IRS guidance ￼.

5 Conclusion

PayLink is more than a time‑clock; it is a full HR and payroll platform designed to serve companies, contractors, S‑corps and non‑profits while meeting federal and state compliance requirements.  By integrating robust time‑tracking, scheduling, HR management and a payroll engine that respects FLSA, IRS and state regulations, the software will reduce administrative burden and legal risk.  Building the platform on Replit using modern web technologies allows rapid development, collaboration and continuous deployment.  Adhering to the compliance obligations and design principles outlined in this document will help ensure PayLink meets its goal of providing a versatile, legally compliant HR, payroll and time‑clock solution.
